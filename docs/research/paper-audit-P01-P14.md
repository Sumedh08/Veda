# Primary-source audit: P01–P14

**Access date:** 2026-10-29 (Asia/Kolkata). This audit checks the first 14 records in docs/research/papers.csv against publisher proceedings, publisher article text, arXiv full text, or the linked project’s own repository/site. The supplied research map was treated as a discovery aid, not evidence. “Source reports” below are statements made by the cited authors; they are not independently replicated results. “Engineering inference” marks a proposed Veda use, not a paper finding.

**Inspection limits:** This was read-only web inspection. No repository was cloned, installed, or executed, and no API calls or paid services were used. Repository review is limited to the pages/files named below; an open-source label does not establish that every dependency, license condition, data right, or current behavior is acceptable for reuse.

## Findings

### P01 — OpenScholar

**Access:** Full article body in Nature’s HTML was readable, including abstract, main results, limitations, data availability and code availability; the publisher PDF URL redirected to an inaccessible authentication endpoint. **Source reports:** OpenScholar combines a 45-million-paper/236-million-passage datastore, specialized retrieval/reranking, citation-backed generation and self-feedback. The article reports improvements over tested baselines on ScholarQABench and 16 scientists’ assessments over 108 expert responses. The abstract reports 2,967 queries/208 answers, while the body describes 3,000 questions/250 answers; use the benchmark’s detailed definitions before quoting a single count. Authors caution that the human-written evaluation subsets are small, rubric scoring uses heuristic weights, and sentence-level citation scoring can be strict. Evaluated fields are computer science, physics, neuroscience and biomedicine, not business/economics. **Code/data:** [OpenScholar implementation](https://github.com/AkariAsai/OpenScholar), [ScholarQABench data](https://github.com/AkariAsai/ScholarQABench/tree/main/data); repository overview and license metadata were viewed, not implementation files. **Engineering inference:** Reuse the ideas of retrieval diversity, reranking, evidence-linked claims and iterative verification; do not infer commercial-domain quality or adopt the 45M-paper infrastructure for a lightweight MVP. [Nature article: abstract, main results, limitations, data/code availability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10072-4); [OpenScholar repository](https://github.com/AkariAsai/OpenScholar).

### P02 — PaperQA2

**Access:** Full arXiv HTML and selected current official repository source pages were inspected (agents/main.py, agents/env.py, and settings); no code was run. **Source reports:** PaperQA2 uses iterative literature search, ranking/selection, citation traversal and answer synthesis with an “insufficient information” option. On the authors’ 248-question biomedical LitQA2 benchmark, it reports 85.2% precision, 66.0% accuracy and 21.9% abstentions; human accuracy was 67.7% on the same task, not statistically different from the system, while human precision was lower. The benchmark is science-specific, automatic scoring includes an LLM-based answer extraction step, and the paper’s quoted $1–$3/query is author-reported for its setup. **Code/data:** [paper-qa repository](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa); paper links supplementary benchmark/evaluation materials. **Engineering inference:** Borrow explicit abstention and an auditable retrieval trail; these results do not validate economic inference or the same cost/accuracy for Veda. [Full paper, especially §§3, 8.2 and 8.4, Data Availability](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740); [agent implementation](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa/blob/main/src/paperqa/agents/main.py); [agent environment](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa/blob/main/src/paperqa/agents/env.py).

### P03 — STORM

**Access:** Full 27-page ACL Anthology PDF and the official current storm_wiki/engine.py page were inspected; source code was not executed. **Source reports:** STORM discovers perspectives, asks questions through simulated expert conversations, curates sources into an outline, then writes an article. Against an outline-driven RAG baseline, the paper reports 25 percentage points more articles rated organized and 10 points more rated good coverage by participating Wikipedia editors. The paper also says machine-written articles still lag revised human articles in neutrality/verifiability and can inherit dominant-source bias or promotional content; experts identify over-association of unrelated facts. **Code/data:** [STORM code](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm), linked in the paper. The inspected engine exposes separable research, outline, article-generation and polishing stages and allows saved intermediate artifacts to be loaded. **Engineering inference:** Multi-angle query generation and visible stages may help coverage; generated “perspectives” are prompts/roles, not evidence of actual expert camps or balanced source representation. [Paper, PDF pp. 1, 7–9](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347.pdf); [engine source](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm/blob/main/knowledge_storm/storm_wiki/engine.py).

### P04 — Co-STORM

**Access:** Full 39-page ACL Anthology PDF. **Source reports:** Co-STORM adds user-observable and user-steerable agent discourse, a dynamic mind map and a cited report. Its small human comparisons report 70% preference over search (n=10) and 78% over a RAG chatbot (n=9); these are participant preferences, not factuality scores. The paper identifies limitations in tailoring to prior knowledge, user control over perspectives/utterance length, English-only retrieval, and added latency. **Code/data:** The paper says resources and code are released in the [STORM repository](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm); code not separately audited here. **Engineering inference:** A persisted research outline and optional user steering are useful patterns; the multi-agent interaction loop is not evidence-backed as a required MVP component. [Paper, PDF pp. 1, 8–9](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.554.pdf).

### P05 — Mind2Report

**Access:** Full arXiv HTML; the paper-linked [USTC repository](https://github.com/ustc-ai4science/Mind2Report) README was viewed, but its implementation was not audited. The paper identifies that same repository as its code/data link; the package’s different owner URL is not supported by this paper. **Source reports:** The design clarifies commercial intent, formulates an outline, recursively searches, distills evidence into research memory, co-evolves memory and outline, and iteratively synthesizes the report. QRC-Eval is described as 200 time-sensitive queries manually crafted by business experts across six domains, with fixed queries, expert keypoints, timestamps and web snapshots. The paper reports averaged results over three runs, with Gemini 2.5 Pro as evaluator and some proprietary systems’ internal tools inaccessible; the reported best scores and ablations are author results on that setup. **Engineering inference:** This is directly relevant to resumable business research: persist clarified intent, evidence records and a revisable outline. It does not independently prove “expert-level” commercial usefulness for Veda or its target market. [Full paper, §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04879); [linked repository](https://github.com/ustc-ai4science/Mind2Report).

### P06 — LangChain Open Deep Research

**Access:** Official GitHub repository banner, README, and license metadata. GitHub states the repository was archived by its owner on 2026-08-21 and is read-only. README describes a configurable LangGraph workflow using multiple model roles and search/MCP choices; it also reports its own benchmark runs and an estimated cost for a full evaluation, which were not independently checked. **Code/data:** [official repository and README](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research); README links an evaluation dataset on LangSmith. No source modules or dependency tree were audited. **Engineering inference:** Keep this as a workflow/reference catalog, not a maintained dependency; archive status verifies the package’s archive claim but does not tell us whether a particular fork is suitable.

### P07 — Agent Laboratory

**Access:** Full arXiv HTML and ACL Anthology proceedings PDF were available; the paper-linked [project site](https://agentlaboratory.github.io/) and its linked [GitHub repository](https://github.com/SamuelSchmidgall/AgentLaboratory) were inspected at README/site level only. **Source reports:** The system’s three phases are literature review, experimentation and report writing, with human checkpoints in co-pilot mode. The authors report an 84% expense decrease versus prior autonomous research methods, but their task is ML research rather than web-based business synthesis. The authors state that literature review failed in 60–80% of runs for the three tested backends, while also reporting whole-workflow subtask success rates above 92%; the definitions/context for these figures are unclear in the accessible report, so the phase figure should not be used without checking the detailed experimental tables. Their limitations section also reports occasional hallucinated experimental results and reliance on LLM reviewers. **Engineering inference:** Staged work and review checkpoints transfer; autonomous code execution, ML experiments and the headline cost reduction do not establish a need or benefit for Veda. [Paper, §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04227); [project page](https://agentlaboratory.github.io/); [repository](https://github.com/SamuelSchmidgall/AgentLaboratory).

### P08 — The AI Scientist

**Access:** Full arXiv HTML. The paper links the [SakanaAI implementation](https://github.com/SakanaAI/AI-Scientist); repository source was not audited or run. **Source reports:** The system generates ideas, edits and executes experiment code, writes papers and uses automated review, evaluated in three ML subfields. The authors explicitly warn against taking generated science at face value: they report implementation errors, insufficient experimental rigor, incorrect/hallucinated results and unsafe code execution, and recommend strict sandboxing. **Engineering inference:** Not a Veda MVP architecture. Its documented failures reinforce keeping arbitrary code execution outside a research-reporting workflow and requiring human validation for empirical claims. [Full paper, abstract, §§3 and 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06292); [code link](https://github.com/SakanaAI/AI-Scientist).

### P09 — DeepTRACE

**Access:** Full 22-page ICLR 2026 conference PDF. **Source reports:** DeepTRACE decomposes report text into statements and constructs citation and factual-support matrices, with eight metrics. Its benchmark uses 303 questions (168 debate, 135 expertise) across nine public search/research systems; most reported metrics use 2,727 system responses. The paper reports citation accuracy of 40–80% across systems and high unsupported-statement rates for some tested systems. The LLM judge’s scores correlate with two human annotators at Pearson r=0.72 for confidence on 100 answers and r=0.62 for 100 factual-support checks; authors say they interpret LLM-judge outputs descriptively, not as ground truth. **Code/data:** The paper says it releases the DeepTrace dataset but the inspected paper did not expose a direct artifact URL; it cites an earlier project repo, which is not necessarily DeepTRACE’s own release. **Engineering inference:** Adopt claim-to-source support matrices as an evaluation pattern, then validate with human review; system snapshots and LLM scoring are not Veda performance evidence. [Paper, PDF pp. 1–2, 4–8](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/ad08767706825033b99122332293033d-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### P10 — ALCE

**Access:** Full 24-page ACL Anthology PDF. **Source reports:** ALCE evaluates cited long-form answers across fluency, correctness and citation quality, with citation recall for whether statements are fully supported and citation precision for irrelevant citations. The paper’s abstract reports that even the best tested models lack complete citation support on ELI5 half the time. The automatic support metric uses an NLI model; the reported agreement with human labels is substantial for citation recall (κ=0.698) and moderate for precision (κ=0.525), so this is an imperfect proxy. **Code/data:** Paper links [ALCE code and data](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/ALCE). **Engineering inference:** A good basis for statement-level citation checks and a local evaluation harness, but its datasets do not establish commercial-report quality. [Paper, PDF pp. 1, 4, 8–9](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398.pdf).

### P11 — SciFact

**Access:** Full 17-page ACL Anthology PDF opened; the abstract and task definition were inspected. **Source reports:** SciFact frames scientific claim verification as retrieving abstracts that support or refute a claim and identifying the rationale; its dataset contains 1.4K expert-written scientific claims paired with evidence-containing abstracts. The abstract says data and code are public. **Code/data:** [AllenAI SciFact repository](https://github.com/allenai/scifact), linked by the paper. **Engineering inference:** Useful precedent for claim/evidence/rationale records and a support/refute outcome. It is a scientific-abstract benchmark, not a general-purpose long-form report evaluator or finance/economics ground-truth set.

### P12 — RARR

**Access:** Full 32-page ACL Anthology PDF and official repository README. **Source reports:** RARR is a post-generation retrieval-and-revision method: find evidence for existing text, revise unsupported claims while preserving the original as much as possible, and produce an attribution report. The paper reports improved attribution and >90% original-intent preservation in its evaluation, and describes task/metric limits: attribution and preservation may trade off, self-evident statements can be penalized, and claim scope matters. **Code/data:** [RARR implementation and evaluation data](https://github.com/anthonywchen/RARR). The README describes query generation, web search, an agreement gate, editing and attribution; it also documents external search/model API dependencies and a dangerous “hallucinate evidence” test option that must not be used for factual attribution. **Engineering inference:** A distinct post-draft audit/repair step is useful; it cannot replace evidence-first collection, and repair should preserve a trace of original and edited claims. [Paper, PDF pp. 1, 7, 9–10](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.910.pdf); [repository README](https://github.com/anthonywchen/RARR).

### P13 — ACM credibility survey

**Access:** Full arXiv HTML preprint (v2, 2025) was read; the ACM DOI/publisher page returned HTTP 403, so the publisher version and final issue metadata were not directly verified. The preprint itself carries the DOI. **Source reports:** This is a survey of 175 NLP works, not a new controlled system evaluation. It groups credibility signals (including factuality/bias, persuasion/fallacies, claims, citations and other text properties) and identifies fragmented research, inconsistent terminology, and lack of a standardized credibility taxonomy/scale. **Code/data:** No implementation or dataset is claimed in the paper. **Engineering inference:** Expose concrete evidence and uncertainty signals rather than compressing them into a universal “source credibility” score; that is a design implication, not a tested result of this survey. [Full preprint, §§1–3 and 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21360); [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3770077) (publisher page inaccessible in this pass).

### P14 — Conflicting Needles in a Haystack

**Access:** Full 16-page ACL Anthology PDF. **Source reports:** The authors embed three mutually exclusive fabricated “needles” in long documents and vary position, repetition, layout and domain relevance. Models usually choose one answer confidently rather than signal uncertainty, with biases toward repetition, recency and surface form. The authors explicitly state there is no correct answer in this setup; limitations include English-only open models up to 70B and fabricated conflicts in fixed Wikipedia-derived haystacks. **Code/data:** No dedicated code/data URL was found in the inspected paper; its references cite the generic Needle-in-a-Haystack repository, not a verified release of this study’s artifacts. **Engineering inference:** Use conflict ordering/repetition as a robustness test, but do not interpret this synthetic setup as a measured rate of real-world evidence-conflict failures. [Paper, PDF pp. 1–2, 9](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1742.pdf).

## Cross-paper engineering takeaways

The primary sources support a lightweight, persisted research workflow built around (1) clarifying user intent and an editable outline, (2) storing retrieved source metadata and evidence passages as durable records, (3) generating each report statement from linked evidence, (4) tracking support, contradiction, uncertainty and source timestamps explicitly, and (5) making a post-draft citation/support audit inspectable. This is an engineering synthesis across the sources, not a result tested by any one paper. The evidence does not justify requiring a large scholarly corpus, multi-agent role-play, autonomous experiment execution, a universal source-credibility score, or automatic repair without preserving the original and its evidence trail.

## Access gaps and unresolved points

- Nature’s full article HTML was accessible, but its linked PDF fetch was blocked by an authentication redirect.
- ACM’s publisher page for P13 returned HTTP 403; only the arXiv preprint full text was reviewed.
- DeepTRACE says its dataset is released, but no direct DeepTRACE artifact URL surfaced in the inspected PDF; availability and license remain unverified.
- Repository code for the linked projects was not executed. The audit does not establish dependency safety, end-to-end reproducibility, data licensing, current API compatibility, or downstream reuse rights beyond the repository metadata/pages viewed.
- Results are not cross-paper comparable: tasks, benchmarks, model versions, judges, source corpora and human evaluation populations differ.
